# Card

`Card` is an interactive container. The card draws the surface, the corner and the
interaction states; everything inside it is a slot, so any content can go there. An
optional alert ring and alert banner mark a status on the whole card. For a section
that needs its own title, caption and actions in a header, use
<a href="/?path=/story/components-displaymodule--playground" target="_top">DisplayModule</a>
— a card has none.

## Anatomy

A card is a surface with one slot in it. The slot is the body — everything the card
shows lives there, and it is also the part that answers the pointer. The card's own
surface is `--surface-level-second` and its corner is `--border-radius-2_5` (10px),
over a resting `0 1px 3px` drop shadow in `--box-shadow-shadow-default`. It clips its
content, so the banner and the body follow the corner without setting one of their own.

An alert ring is drawn at the card's edge, over everything. An alert banner sits above
the body, flush to the top edge, and takes the card's top corners.

<DocCaption>{`banner set${TEXT_SEPARATOR}status = "info"`}</DocCaption>

<Canvas of={CardStories.Anatomy} />

The body holds a small, even inset on all four sides — `padding` defaults to 4px,
because a card most often carries a single
<a href="/?path=/docs/components-listitem-listitem--docs" target="_top">ListItem</a>
and the inset is the seat for it. Pass a number for px, or any CSS length or token.

The inset belongs to the body, so the interaction fill covers it too and reaches the
card's corners. Without that, a thin untinted frame would show around the content on
every hover. The card keeps its own surface in every state, and the body carries the
fill on top of it through the `--card-body-fill` custom property.

## When to use

Use a card when the whole container is one target — the person taps it to open or pick
the thing it shows. Do not use a card for a block that only holds content and has
nothing to tap. A plain container is enough there, and a card's shadow and hover
promise an action that never happens.

<Canvas of={CardStories.WhenToUse} />

Do not use a card when the section needs a title, a caption or actions along the top.
DisplayModule is built for that, and it carries the same alert ring and banner.

## Holding a ListItem

A card most often holds one ListItem, and the body's inset is sized for exactly that.
Set the row to not clickable with `isClickable={false}`. The card is the target, so a
clickable row inside it would be a second target over the same area.

In code the two do not merely compete — the row wins. The card excludes slot hover
with `:not(:has(.body :is(button, a, [role="button"], …):hover))`, so a clickable row
takes the pointer and the card stops tinting over its own area.

<Canvas of={CardStories.HoldingListItem} />

The row's right slot is the exception. An open marker there is a plain chevron and not
a button — `ListItemSlotIcon` renders a non-interactive span, so it tells the person
that the card opens something and adds no target of its own. An action in that slot, a
menu button for example, is a real target: it does its own job, and the slot exclusion
above keeps it from opening the card.

## Alert ring

An alert ring marks the whole card with a status. It is a 1px inset `box-shadow` drawn
at the card's edge, over the body and the banner, so it reads as a property of the card
and not of its content. Drawing it as an inset shadow rather than a border keeps the
card's size unchanged when it appears.

There are four statuses, set with `status`: info (`--blue-9`), success (`--jade-9`),
warning (`--amber-9`) and error (`--tomato-9`).

<DocCaption>{`alertRing${TEXT_SEPARATOR}status = "info", "success", "warning", "error"`}</DocCaption>

<Canvas of={CardStories.AlertRing} />

A ring can be used on its own with `alertRing`. A banner cannot — passing `banner`
always draws a matching ring.

## Alert banner

An alert banner carries a message about the card. It is an
<a href="/?path=/story/components-alertbanner--playground" target="_top">AlertBanner</a>
in its `banner` type, and it takes its colour from the same `status` as the ring, so the
two always agree — you pass only its content and actions.

The banner is not part of the card's target. It is rendered as a sibling of the body,
not a child, and it stops its own clicks, so tapping it does nothing to the card. The
card's fill stops below it, which is why the banner holds one colour in every state.
Its own link and dismiss button are separate targets.

## Loading and errors

The card has no loading look of its own. The body is a slot, so the card cannot know
what is loading inside it — `loading` only makes the card non-interactive and shows the
native progress cursor. Draw the loading content in the slot yourself, with skeletons
that match the real content.

When the content cannot be loaded at all, `error` replaces the body with the
<a href="/?path=/story/components-emptystate--playground" target="_top">EmptyState</a>
that Dialog and DisplayModule use, and the card stops being interactive. Wire `onRetry`
to the Reload button.

## States

Six states. The surface and the corner never change; what moves is the body's fill, the
shadow and the edge. Default and disabled keep the resting shadow. Hovered, pressed and
focused drop it, so the card sits flat while it is engaged.

Hover is wrapped in `@media (hover: hover)`, so a touch device never leaves a sticky
hover behind after a tap.

| State | Body fill | Shadow | Edge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| default | `--surface-interactive-default` | `0 1px 3px` | — |
| hovered | `--surface-interactive-hover` | none | — |
| pressed | `--surface-interactive-press` | none | — |
| focused | `--surface-interactive-hover` | none | 2px `--gray-12` |
| dragging | `--surface-interactive-default` | `0 8px 16px` | 1px `--gray-12` |
| disabled | `--surface-interactive-default` | `0 1px 3px` | —, at 30% opacity |

## Accessibility

The card is one stop in the tab order, and Enter and Space activate it. The body takes
`role="button"` and `tabIndex={0}` only when the card is interactive and an `onClick` is
passed — a card without a handler is a plain container and stays out of the tab order.

It announces itself as a button. Anything interactive inside it is its own stop after
the card — an action in the row's right slot, and the banner's link and dismiss button.

When the content does not already name the card — a thumbnail with no text, for
example — give the card an accessible name that says what it opens, with `aria-label`
on the card.
